NAME

markwrite - edit a document in the notation it was written in

SYNOPSIS

markwrite [options] [file]

DESCRIPTION

markwrite opens a document and shows it two ways at once: as the page it will print and as the text it is written in. The markup stays in the stream. A tag in XML, a hash in Markdown, a request line in roff and a sawtooth bracket in LMNL are each a token over the characters that represent them, and what the document means is derived from the tokens rather than kept beside them.

An edit is made to the stream and nothing else. The tokens after it move with the text, the tokens inside it are read again, and everything the page was made of that the edit did not touch is kept. That is what lets a keystroke in a long book cost what the paragraph it falls in costs.

Seven notations are read and written: XML, HTML, Markdown, LMNL, and the man, ms and me macro packages of roff, with eqn for the mathematics in the last three. Word and OpenDocument files are imported and exported through their own readers and writers rather than as notations.

OPTIONS

--look name
Open the document under the named stylesheet rather than the one it carries. A look is a stylesheet that ships with the application; the names are those the View menu lists.
--source
Open with the source pane showing and the printed pane hidden, which is how a document is opened for proofreading its markup.
--export kind
Write the document out as pdf, docx or odt and exit without opening a window.
--version
Print the version and exit.

FILES

~/Library/Application Support/Markwrite/settings
Where the menus stand and whether documents save themselves, one setting to a line, readable and mendable by hand.

NOTES

A request this page's reader does not know shows nothing and is kept as it was written, so a page from the shelf opens, prints and saves back byte for byte. Registers, strings, conditionals and macros of a page's own are among what is kept and not read: a page that represents its words through them shows less than man(1) would.

The escapes for glyphs are read by name, so “the em dash — and the bullet •” show as the characters they name, and \& stands for the zero-width space it always has.

EXAMPLES

Open a Markdown chapter under the Book look:

$ markwrite --look Book chapter.md

Write a manual page out as a Word document without opening it:

$ markwrite --export docx markwrite.1
